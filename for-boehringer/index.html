01 Scenario-based learning · you decide

The Brief

Your situation

You lead learning for global Medicine teams. A new safety-reporting workflow goes live in six weeks. Four thousand people, forty countries, wildly different levels of English. You have one internal designer - me - and budget for a single external supplier.

Three decisions. There are no trick questions, but there are expensive answers.

How I would actually run it

Six weeks, one designer, one supplier

  1. Week 1 - Scope, don't designSit with the subject matter expert and split the content into "must be able to do", "must know", and "can look up". Most of a 62-slide deck is the third category. That becomes a one-page job aid, not a module.
  2. Week 2 - Prototype one branchBuild a single realistic decision scenario, in low fidelity, and put it in front of eight real users. Cheap to throw away. This is where the design gets decided, not in the authoring tool.
  3. Weeks 3 to 4 - Produce in parallelInteractive module in-house. Video narration through an AI presenter so all forty markets ship on the same day and any correction is a re-render, not a re-shoot. Supplier takes translation QA and localisation review - the one thing that genuinely does not scale in-house.
  4. Week 5 - Accessibility and review passCaptions, transcripts, keyboard path, contrast, screen reader labels. Done as a gate before sign-off, not as a retrofit afterwards - retrofitting accessibility costs roughly three times more.
  5. Week 6 - Launch with the measurement already wiredCompletion to the LMS, interaction data to the LXP, and one agreed business metric checked at day 30. If we cannot name that metric before launch, we are not ready to launch.
This is not a hypothetical process. It is the same sequence I use on campaign and packaging work, where a late change is equally expensive - only the deliverable changes.

03 One script · three productions

Choosing the Medium

Anyone can generate an AI presenter now. The value is not in operating the tool - it is in knowing which of these three a given piece of content deserves, and what each one costs you when the content changes in six months.

3D · Motion

Built, not filmed

Blender and Unreal. For anything that cannot be photographed: a mechanism, a molecule, a process inside a device, an abstraction. Most learning teams outsource this. I do it in-house.

Use it when The subject is invisible, internal or conceptual - and a stock photo would be a lie.
Cost to change
AI presenter

Synthesia / HeyGen

One script, forty markets, same launch date. A correction six months later is a re-render that takes an afternoon instead of a reshoot that takes a quarter.

Use it when Content is procedural, multilingual and will change. Not when the message needs a person the audience must trust - a real leader, a real patient. An avatar delivering that reads as evasive.
Cost to change
Live capture

Camera and craft

Slow, costly, and irreplaceable for the few messages that only work when a real human says them on camera. Knowing this is the small category - not the default - is most of the skill.

Use it when Trust, culture or leadership voice is the actual payload - and the content will stay valid for years.
Cost to change
Transcript & audio description

04 Debrief · the honest part

What I Am and Am Not

The gap, stated plainly

I am not a three-year Articulate Storyline specialist

Your posting asks for three years of e-learning development in an authoring-tool-first environment. My background is campaign, brand, 3D and regulated packaging design, not a pharma L&D department. I would rather you hear that from me now than discover a rehearsed answer to it in an interview.

What comes with me instead

The parts that take years, not weeks

  • Regulated-environment discipline. At SECURIKETT I designed labels and packaging in a technically driven, precision-critical environment. Version control, tolerance for review cycles, and understanding that a detail being wrong is not a style question.
  • Stakeholder-heavy campaign delivery. At GGK MullenLowe: Österreichische Lotterien, Puntigamer, Gösser, OMV. Brand consistency across every channel, on fixed deadlines, with many opinions in the room.
  • Production most learning teams outsource. Blender, Unreal, motion, 3D product visualisation, full post pipeline. This is the expensive line in a supplier quote, and it is in-house with me.
  • An international practice that keeps the craft honest. SuperRare Open Call winner; work exhibited in Rome and on Times Square, New York.
  • Vienna, and three working languages. Spanish native, English fluent, German growing. Global content that has to land in more than one culture is not an abstraction to me.
  • And this module. Scenario branching, progress persistence, an xAPI event model, WCAG 2.2 AA, three languages, my own 3D work - designed, written, translated and coded from an empty folder, by one person, to apply for one job.

Storyline is a tool. I have learned harder ones, faster, for lower stakes than this.
